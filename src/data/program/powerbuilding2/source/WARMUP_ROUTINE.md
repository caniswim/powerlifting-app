# Powerbuilding Phase 2.0 - Warm-Up Routine
## Complete Warm-Up Protocol (From the Program Guide)

---

## Why Warming Up Matters

Warming up properly is especially important on this program due to the heavy loads used on compound lifts. A good warm-up accomplishes three things:

- **Raises core body temperature** — improves performance and lowers injury risk. If you train early in the morning, 5-10 minutes of low-to-moderate cardio beforehand is especially helpful.
- **Increases muscle activation** — dynamic drills (active movements through a joint's range of motion) improve force output more than passive stretching. Stay mentally engaged with the muscles you're activating rather than just going through the motions.
- **Reduces muscle soreness** — a few minutes of light foam rolling before training can improve range of motion and help prevent injury.

---

## Part 1: The General Warm-Up (Do This Every Session)

| Exercise | Sets | Reps/Time | Notes |
|----------|------|-----------|-------|
| Low intensity cardio | N/A | 5-10 min | Any machine, elevate heart rate to ~100-135 bpm |
| Foam rolling / lacrosse ball | N/A | 2-3 min | Foam roll large muscle groups (quads, lats, calves). Optionally use a lacrosse ball on smaller areas (pecs, delts, hamstrings) |
| Front/Back Leg Swing | 1 | 12 each leg | Dynamic hip mobility |
| Side/Side Leg Swing | 1 | 12 each leg | Dynamic hip mobility |
| Standing Glute Squeeze | 1 | 15 sec | Squeeze your glutes as hard as possible |
| Prone Trap Raise | 1 | 15 reps | Mind-muscle connection with mid-back |
| Cable External Rotation (Optional) | 1 | 15 each side | Rotator cuff activation |
| Cable Internal Rotation (Optional) | 1 | 15 each side | Rotator cuff activation |
| Overhead Shrug (Optional) | 1 | 15 reps | Light squeeze on traps at the top of each rep |

## Part 2: The Specific Pyramid Warm-Up (Before Primary Exercises Only)

Before your first exercise for a given body part (i.e. the "Primary Exercise" of that session — usually the first movement listed on each training day), pyramid up in weight with 3-4 light, progressively heavier sets before starting your working sets. This extensive warm-up is **only needed for Primary Exercises**, not every exercise in the session.

### Example — Based on Absolute Weight
(Working up to 4 sets of 350 lbs x 5 reps on the squat)

| Set | Load | Reps |
|-----|------|------|
| 1 | Bar (45 lbs) | 15 |
| 2 | 135 lbs | 5 |
| 3 | 225 lbs | 4 |
| 4 | 275 lbs | 3 |
| 5 | 315 lbs | 2 |
| — | **Begin working sets** | 350 lbs x 5 |

### Example — Based on %1RM

| Set | Load | Reps |
|-----|------|------|
| 1 | Bar (45 lbs) | 15 |
| 2 | ~40% 1RM | 5 |
| 3 | ~50% 1RM | 4 |
| 4 | ~60% 1RM | 3 |
| 5 | ~70-75% 1RM | 2 |
| — | **Begin working sets** | — |

---

## Notes on Warm-Up Sets & Customizing Your Warm-Up

- The "Warm-Up Sets" column in each workout table is a **suggested** number, not a fixed rule. If you're working up to a very heavy top set, you may need 5-6 warm-up sets to feel fully ready. If you're less experienced/strong, 3 sets may be enough.
- You're free to add more warm-up drills to the protocol above — just keep the total warm-up time to roughly 10-20 minutes. Don't rush it, but don't overdo it either.
- If you're feeling more sore than usual, an extra 3-5 minutes of foam rolling either before or after your session can help.
